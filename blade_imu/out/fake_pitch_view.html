How to use
  1. Open a log: pick a WT901 .txt above or drop it on the page. The first time, the page downloads Python (~20 MB) to run the analysis, so it needs internet and takes a few seconds.
  2. Set the constants: at least sensor radius r (distance from the rotation axis to the sensor). Masses and radii are only needed for torque; wind speed only for tip speed ratio.
  3. Play or drag the time slider. Click any chart to jump to that moment. The drawings, the current values and the transform matrix follow the time cursor.
  4. Relationships (bottom): choose X and Y to see how two quantities move together, e.g. pitch vs rpm. Pearson r (−1…1) says how close they are to a straight line; the fit gives the equation y = … and R² (share of Y's variation the equation explains; for a line R² = r²).
  5. Download CSV for every computed column with the current constants.

Reading the charts (Plotly)

  • Hover over a line to read exact values at that time.
  • Click a point to move the time cursor there (drawings, values and transform update).
  • Drag across a chart to zoom into that box; drag along the edge of an axis to zoom only that axis. Zooming time on one chart zooms every time chart, so the pairs stacked under each other (computed above, measured below) stay aligned.
  • Double-click a chart to zoom back out to everything.
  • Click a legend entry to hide or show that line; double-click it to show only that line (double-click again for all).
  • The toolbar at the top right of each chart (appears on hover) has pan, zoom in/out, autoscale, reset and download as PNG.

Limits to keep in mind

  • The gyro saturates at ±2000 °/s (≈ 333 rpm). Above that, speed comes from the centripetal acceleration and is only as good as r; the accelerometer itself saturates at ±16 g (r = 0.1 m → ≈ 378 rpm).
  • Samples arrive at ~10 Hz. Near 600 rpm (and 1200, 1800 …) the rotor turns a whole number of times between samples, so the blade angle from gravity is unreliable there.
  • Pitch is seen only while the rotor spins above ~30 rpm and the gyro is not saturated; it is the change since the start of the record (set blade pitch at start for absolute values).
  • Grey lines are raw sensor readings (x dark, y mid, z light); coloured lines are computed. Blank stretches mean "cannot be computed here", not zero.
What the values mean
sensor frame x, y, zThe three axes printed on the sensor. They turn with the blade. All raw readings are in this frame.
u (rotation axis)Direction the rotor spins about, written in sensor x, y, z. Found as the dominant direction of the gyro readings (SVD); corrected for pitch over time.
e1 (radial)From the axis out along the blade to the sensor, found from the direction of the centripetal acceleration. Pitch turns the blade about this line.
e2 (chord-wise)u × e1: the direction the sensor moves as the rotor turns.
speed (rpm), ωSpin rate about u. rpm = (°/s) ÷ 6; ω in rad/s = rpm × 2π / 60.
αAngular acceleration: how fast ω changes (rad/s²). Positive = speeding up.
net torque τ = I·αTotal torque on the rotor (wind minus generator minus friction). Zero at constant speed even when the wind pushes hard; the wind's own torque needs power data (τ = P/ω).
I (moment of inertia)Resistance to spin-up: n·m·R²/3 for the blades (uniform rods from the axis to R) + ½·m·r² for hub and generator rotor (solid disk) + I₀ for anything else.
blade angle θWhere the blade is on its circle. 0° = sensor at the bottom (from gravity) when the axis is horizontal; otherwise 0° = start of the record.
axis elevationHow far the rotation axis is tilted up from horizontal (°).
axis driftAngle between the spin direction over the last ~2 s and the pitch-corrected axis: yaw, shaft wobble, blade flex.
wobble e1, e2Rotation rate across the axis (°/s), split into radial and chord-wise parts, with the pitch rate removed.
blade pitchTwist of the blade about its own span (e1), from the IMU. Change since the start + blade pitch at start.
tip speed ratio λBlade tip speed ÷ wind speed = ωR / V. Needs wind speed V.
centripetal ω²rAcceleration toward the axis at the sensor, from turning in a circle.
tangential αrAcceleration along the direction of motion, from speeding up or slowing down.
gyro clippedA gyro axis hit ±2000 °/s; that sample's spin comes from the accelerometer instead ("speed source").

Sensor → rotor transform at this moment

Each row is a rotor direction written in sensor x, y, z. Multiplying a raw reading by this matrix gives its rotor components. It is a pure rotation (rows are perpendicular unit vectors), so the reverse is the transpose. e1 stays fixed on the sensor; u and e2 turn with blade pitch.

Blue: rotor speed (rpm). Light orange: speed from the centripetal acceleration (shown when the gyro saturates). Red dots: gyro saturated, speed taken from the accelerometer.

Raw gyro x, y, z (°/s), exactly as logged (x dark grey, y mid, z light). The spin above is these three combined along the axis u (see the transform panel).

Green: α, the rate of change of the speed (rad/s²). Light green: net torque I·α (right axis), the same curve scaled by I.

Rate of change of each raw gyro axis (rad/s², same smoothing as α). The sensor does not measure angular acceleration; this is the derivative of the raw readings.

Black: blade angle used in the drawing (gyro integrated, held to the gravity angle). Grey dots: angle from gravity alone, sample by sample.

The sensor's own AngleX/Y/Z (Euler angles it computes internally), for comparison. They describe the sensor's attitude, not the blade angle, and jump near ±90°.

Orange: centripetal acceleration estimated from the accelerometer. Teal: part along the rotation axis (gravity × sin(elevation)).

Raw accelerometer x, y, z (g), exactly as logged. Includes gravity, centripetal and tangential acceleration mixed together.

Gravity part of the raw reading (g), estimated. An accelerometer at rest reads +1 g pointing up, so this vector has length ≈ 1 and turns as the blade turns.

Raw minus gravity (g): what the sensor feels from motion alone, i.e. centripetal + tangential acceleration + vibration.

Pink: blade pitch from the IMU. Blank where the gyro is saturated or the rotor is too slow to see the axis.

Teal: rotation axis tilt from horizontal. Light teal: how far the short-term spin direction strays from the axis.

Purple: rotation across the axis (°/s), radial e1 (dark) and chord-wise e2 (light), pitch rate removed. Ideally near zero.
